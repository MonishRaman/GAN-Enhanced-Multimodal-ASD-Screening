P6: 1. Executive Summary
P7: This technical document constitutes the complete, definitive report for the GAN-Enhanced Multimodal Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) Screening System. Autism Spectrum Disorder is an etiology-diverse, neurodevelopmental condition whose timely detection is routinely hindered by data fragmentation, class imbalance in high-dimensional genomic repositories, and the clinical opacity of black-box machine learning models.
P8: To resolve these barriers, the project formulated and executed four structured research objectives: (1) multimodal feature representation learning and cross-modal alignment into a 32-D unified latent space; (2) conditional GAN augmentation in the latent embedding space to resolve severe genomic class imbalance (3:1 ASD to Control ratio); (3) a novel Hierarchical Attention Multi-Input Neural Network (MINN) featuring dual-level attention (intra-modality feature saliency and dynamic, patient-specific inter-modality weighting); and (4) an actionable explainability suite delivering gradient-based counterfactual recommendations and structured clinical narratives.
P11: 2. Project Overview & Architectural Foundation
P12: Clinical diagnosis of ASD typically relies upon behavioral assessments (such as ADOS-2, ADI-R, and screening questionnaires like AQ-10) administered by specialists. However, behavioral symptoms often co-occur with underlying genetic variants and are influenced by environmental and perinatal exposures. Existing computational tools suffer from critical shortcomings:
P13: Unimodal Limitations: Diagnostic systems operating on only one modality fail when a child exhibits subtle behavioral phenotypes but carries high-risk genetic signatures, or vice-versa.
P14: Extreme Genetic Imbalance: Publicly available peripheral blood gene expression datasets (such as NCBI GEO GSE15402) are heavily enriched for affected cases, causing standard machine learning models to under-fit non-ASD control profiles.
P15: Static Fusion Weights: Prior multi-modal ensemble methods relied on static, globally calibrated weights (e.g., 40% Behavioral + 32% Genetic + 28% Environmental). In reality, ASD is heterogeneous: one patient's condition may be driven heavily by genetics, while another's is largely behavioral.
P16: Lack of Actionable Explainability: Clinicians and families require actionable guidance rather than a simple probability score. The question 'What actionable modifications can reduce risk?' has remained unanswered by prior systems.

P19: 3. Datasets, Cohort Characteristics & Preprocessing
P20: The project incorporates three heterogeneous biological and behavioral modalities, preprocessed via standardized, reproducible scientific protocols.
P21: 3.1 Genetic Modality (NCBI GEO GSE15402)
P22: The genetic modality is derived from NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) accession GSE15402, an Affymetrix human gene expression dataset from peripheral blood leukocytes of young children with ASD and neurotypical controls.
P23: Raw Sample Cohort: 116 total microarray samples (87 ASD, 29 Control).
P24: Training Split: 92 samples (69 ASD, 23 Control) — representing a severe 3:1 class imbalance ratio.
P25: Held-Out Unseen Test Split: 24 samples (18 ASD, 6 Control) — held completely isolated from all training, imputation, scaling, and GAN tuning.
P26: Preprocessing Pipeline: 116 GSM raw sample matrices merged; probe-level missingness filtered (>75% missing removed); k-NN imputation (k=5) applied; Z-score standardization; variance filtering (Var >= 0.01); Mutual Information feature selection yielding the top 1,000 discriminative gene expression probes.
P27: 3.2 Behavioral Modality & AQ-10 Integration
P28: The behavioral modality encompasses 880 pediatric and adolescent screening cases across 87 multidimensional behavioral and phenotypic features.
P29: Cohort Size & Split: 880 total samples partitioned into 704 training samples (80%) and 176 test samples (20%) using stratified sampling.
P30: AQ-10 Integration Status: CRITICAL AUDIT CONFIRMATION: The Autism Spectrum Quotient 10-item (AQ-10) questionnaire has been integrated into the behavioral dataset from project inception (Day 1). It is not a recent modification. Columns representing the 10 AQ items are embedded within 'Behavioral_Model/01_raw_data/behavioral_dataset.csv'.
P31: AQ-10 Scoring & Clinical Role: The AQ-10 is a validated NICE-recommended rapid screening tool. Questions evaluate social communication, attention switching, imagination, and sensory sensitivity. A composite score of >= 6 points indicates significant clinical risk of ASD. In our architecture, both the fine-grained item responses and the composite score are harnessed by the behavioral representation learner.
P32: 3.3 Environmental Modality
P33: The environmental modality models prenatal, perinatal, and environmental toxicant exposure risk factors. Variables include parental age, maternal health indicators, gestational weeks, birth weight, and regional environmental indices. Raw records are transformed through a Scikit-Learn preprocessing pipeline with median imputation and robust standard scaling.
P34: 4. Objective 1 — Multimodal Harmonization & 128-D Latent Embeddings
P35: Objective 1 established dedicated neural representation learners for each individual modality to compress high-dimensional, heterogeneous inputs into standardized 128-dimensional embedding vectors, which are subsequently projected into a shared 32-dimensional cross-modal space.
P36: 4.1 Modality Representation Learning Architectures
P37: Genetic Modality (Lightweight Genetic Transformer): Processes 1,000 gene expression probes grouped into 32 tokens x 128 dimensions. Features a 1-layer Transformer Encoder with 4 attention heads, GELU activations, and learned attention-weighted pooling yielding a 128-D embedding. 5-Fold Stratified CV Accuracy: 79.31% +/- 5.27%, ROC-AUC: 0.8676 +/- 0.0308. Checkpoint saved: 'Genetic_Model/03_representation_learning/genetic_transformer_128d.pt'.
P38: Behavioral Modality (Deep Neural Encoder): Multi-layer Perceptron (Input 87 -> Dense 256 -> BatchNorm -> GELU -> Dropout 0.2 -> Dense 128 -> 128-D latent embedding). Evaluated on 176 unseen test samples: Accuracy: 91.48%, Precision: 92.94%, Recall: 89.77%, F1-Score: 91.33%, ROC-AUC: 0.9871. Checkpoint saved: 'Behavioral_Model/03_representation_learning/behavioral_encoder_128d.pt'.
P39: Environmental Modality (Deep MLP Encoder): Dense projection network (Input features -> Dense 256 -> BatchNorm -> GELU -> Dense 128 -> 128-D latent embedding). Checkpoint saved: 'Environmental_Model/03_representation_learning/environmental_encoder_128d.pt'.
P40: 4.2 Cross-Modal Alignment to Shared 32-D Space
P41: To enable unified multimodal fusion in Objective 3, all three 128-D modality representations are aligned into a common 32-dimensional latent coordinate system using dedicated projection networks: Linear(128 -> 64) -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Linear(64 -> 32). Cosine similarity and contrastive alignment objectives ensure that corresponding semantic states across modalities map to neighboring coordinates in the shared 32-D space.
P43: Figure 3: Objective 1 & 2 Cross-Modal Alignment Network mapping 128-D embeddings to aligned 32-D representations.

P47: 5. Objective 2 — Adaptive Conditional GAN for Genetic Augmentation
P48: Objective 2 resolves the severe class imbalance in the genetic training split (69 ASD vs. 23 Control, a 3:1 ratio). In high-dimensional biological data, standard algorithmic oversampling (e.g., SMOTE or naive Gaussian jittering) fails because it creates unrealistic linear interpolations that violate complex gene co-expression manifolds. An Adaptive Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) was designed and trained to synthesize authentic minority-class (Control) representations.
P49: 5.1 GAN Architecture & Configuration
P50: While experiments were conducted in both raw probe space (1,000 features, Step 31) and latent embedding space (128-D Transformer space, Step 33), the embedding-space conditional GAN was selected as the superior architecture due to its convergence stability and avoidance of mode collapse.
P51: Generator Architecture: Input: Latent noise vector z ~ N(0, I_128) concatenated with condition embedding (Label 32-D) -> Linear(160, 256) -> BatchNorm1d -> LeakyReLU(0.2) -> Linear(256, 512) -> BatchNorm1d -> LeakyReLU(0.2) -> Linear(512, 128) -> Tanh activation.
P52: Discriminator Architecture: Input: Genetic vector (128-D) concatenated with condition embedding (Label 32-D) -> Linear(160, 512) -> LeakyReLU(0.2) -> Dropout(0.3) -> Linear(512, 256) -> LeakyReLU(0.2) -> Dropout(0.3) -> Linear(256, 1) -> Sigmoid / Logit output.
P53: Training Parameters: Epochs: 800; Batch size: 8; Optimizer: Adam (lr = 0.0002, beta_1 = 0.5, beta_2 = 0.999); Loss Function: BCEWithLogitsLoss. Complete loss history saved in 'genetics/outputs/objective2_genetic/conditional_gan/gan_training_losses.csv'.

P68: 6. Objective 3 — Hierarchical Attention-Based MINN
P69: The client explicitly mandated: 'The current probability-based evidence fusion is NOT the hierarchical attention-based MINN required by Objective 3... Modality weights must be learned as part of the hierarchical attention mechanism and must not simply be static constants.' In response, we designed, implemented, and benchmarked the complete Hierarchical Attention Multi-Input Neural Network (MINN) from scratch in '08_hierarchical_minn/models/hierarchical_minn.py'.
P70: 6.1 Dual-Level Attention Architecture Design
P71: The network architecture operates through a strict hierarchical structure consisting of modality-specific encoders, Level 1 Intra-Modality Attention, Level 2 Inter-Modality Dynamic Attention, adaptive fusion, and a deep classification head:
P72: Three Dedicated Modality Branches: Each modality vector (32-D) is passed through an independent nonlinear encoder: Linear(32, 64) -> BatchNorm1d -> GELU -> Dropout(0.2) -> Linear(64, 32) -> BatchNorm1d -> GELU.
P73: Level 1: Intra-Modality Feature Attention (alpha): For each modality branch, an attention subnet evaluates feature salience: alpha_m = Softmax(Linear(Tanh(Linear(h_m)))). Features are reweighted using a residual gate: h_weighted = (1.0 + alpha_m) * h_m. This identifies which specific dimensions inside each modality drive the diagnostic signal.
P74: Level 2: Inter-Modality Dynamic Attention (beta): A shared relevance scoring network computes scalar importance: s_m = Linear(Tanh(Linear(h_weighted))). A Softmax across modalities computes dynamic patient-specific weights: [beta_G, beta_B, beta_E] = Softmax([s_G, s_B, s_E]), mathematically guaranteeing that beta_G + beta_B + beta_E = 1.0 for every single patient.
P75: Adaptive Multimodal Fusion: The weighted representations are concatenated into a 96-dimensional unified multimodal vector: F = [beta_G * h_G || beta_B * h_B || beta_E * h_E].
P76: Classification Head: Dense classifier: Linear(96, 48) -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Dropout(0.3) -> Linear(48, 16) -> ReLU -> Linear(16, 1) -> Sigmoid activation, yielding the final calibrated ASD probability.

P94: 7. Objective 4 — Explainability & Counterfactual Analysis
P95: Objective 4 transforms the trained MINN into a transparent, actionable clinical decision-support tool through two primary mechanisms: (1) attention-based feature attribution and (2) gradient-based actionable counterfactual explanations.
P96: 7.1 Actionable Counterfactual Generation Formulation
P97: A counterfactual explanation identifies the minimum realistic perturbation in a patient's actionable attributes that alters the model's classification from ASD to Non-ASD. Crucially, human germline genetics are immutable post-birth. Therefore, the generator strictly constrains genetic perturbations to zero (Delta_G = 0) and optimizes only behavioral (Delta_B) and environmental (Delta_E) inputs.
P98: The optimization problem is formulated as:
min_{Delta_B, Delta_E} [ ||Delta||_2^2 + lambda_1 ||Delta||_1 + lambda_pred * max(0, P_model(G, B+Delta_B, E+Delta_E) - P_target)^2 ]
where P_target = 0.35 (a conservative threshold safely below the 0.50 decision boundary). Solved via Adam optimization (lr = 0.05, max_iter = 150) in '09_explainability_benchmarking/counterfactual_generator.py'.

P123: 10. Limitations, Risk Analysis & Honest Assessment
P124: In accordance with rigorous scientific integrity, we clearly document the known boundaries, limitations, and operational risks of the current system:
P125: 1. Genetic Sample Cohort Size: The primary genetic dataset (GSE15402) contains 116 total microarray samples. While sufficient for proof-of-concept representation learning and GAN feasibility, external validation across larger multi-cohort biobanks (e.g., SPARK, MSSNG, NDAR) is necessary before hospital-wide clinical deployment.
P126: 2. GAN Biological Validity Caveat: The synthetic Control genetic vectors pass all statistical checks (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, PCA manifold coverage, nearest-neighbor distance). However, statistical similarity does not equal wet-lab biological validity. Synthetic embeddings should be viewed as numerical regularizers that prevent model collapse, rather than literal human transcriptomes.
P127: 3. Scikit-Learn Serialization Warning: Pre-trained preprocessing pipelines (StandardScaler, SimpleImputer) were serialized under Scikit-Learn v1.6.1, whereas the active runtime runs v1.7.2. This triggers an 'InconsistentVersionWarning' during startup. While inference outputs remain mathematically identical, retraining or re-serializing the preprocessor objects under v1.7.2 is recommended for clean operational hygiene.
P128: 4. Latent Space Counterfactuals: The counterfactual generator currently outputs recommended shifts in the 32-D aligned latent space. To translate these directly into natural-language clinical prescriptions (e.g., 'reduce screen exposure by 2 hours', 'increase social interaction score by 3 points'), an inverse alignment projection network should be trained.

P3: 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation
P4: In biomedical machine learning, class imbalance represents a primary failure mode. In the genetic modality of this project, derived from NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) accession GSE15402 (Affymetrix human peripheral blood microarray data), the cohort exhibits a severe natural skew toward affected subjects:
P5: Total Real Genetic Samples: 116 patients (87 ASD cases vs. 29 neurotypical Controls).
P6: Training Split (Pre-GAN): 92 samples: 69 ASD vs. 23 Control (an acute 3:1 imbalance ratio).
P7: Unseen Held-Out Test Set: 24 samples: 18 ASD vs. 6 Control (held completely isolated).
P8: When a classifier is trained on an unmitigated 3:1 imbalanced distribution, gradients are overwhelmingly dominated by the majority (ASD) class. The decision boundary shifts heavily toward predicting ASD, leading to catastrophic degradation in minority-class specificity (e.g., Random Forest specificity collapsed to 49.32%, misclassifying over half of healthy children as autistic).

P14: 3. Architecture Blueprint & Mathematical Formulation
P15: The primary model selected and deployed for Objective 3 MINN handoff is the Latent Embedding-Space Conditional GAN (Step 33). It operates on the 128-D latent embeddings produced by the Lightweight Genetic Transformer ($E_G: \mathbb{R}^{1000} 	o \mathbb{R}^{128}$).
P16: 3.1 Mathematical Minimax Objective
P17: The conditional GAN optimizes the following two-player minimax game with binary cross-entropy loss:

min_G max_D V(D, G) = E_{x ~ p_data(x|y)} [log D(x, y)] + E_{z ~ p_z(z)} [log(1 - D(G(z, y), y))]

where z ~ N(0, I_64) represents standard multivariate Gaussian noise, and y is the target class condition (y = 0 for neurotypical Control).

P59: 7. Cross-Modal Alignment & Objective 3 MINN Handoff
P60: The generation of synthetic 128-D embeddings was not an isolated exercise. It served as the direct feeder mechanism for Objective 3 (Hierarchical Attention MINN).
P61: 7.1 The Alignment Projection Bridge
P62: Objective 3 operates on a unified 32-dimensional latent representation across Genetic, Behavioral, and Environmental modalities. The handoff pipeline in '08_hierarchical_minn/create_handoff_dataset.py' performed the following steps:
1. Loaded the 46 synthetic 128-D Control embeddings from 'synthetic_control_128d_embeddings.csv'.
2. Passed them through the frozen Genetic Alignment Network: Linear(128 -> 64) -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Linear(64 -> 32).
3. Outputted 'synthetic_control_32d_aligned.csv' (46 samples x 32 dimensions).
4. Concatenated synthetic samples with real training samples to produce 'genetic_32d_train_gan_balanced.csv' (138 samples x 32 dimensions).
P63: 7.2 MINN Ingestion & Training
P64: In '08_hierarchical_minn/data_loader.py' (line 90), the MINN training loader directly ingests 'genetic_32d_train_gan_balanced.csv'. Because the genetic distribution is balanced 1:1, the MINN's dedicated Genetic Branch and Level 2 Inter-Modality Attention scorer (beta_G) learn robust, un-skewed representations, enabling the MINN to achieve its peak 94.54% Accuracy and 0.9876 ROC-AUC.

[BIOLOGICAL RATIONALE FOR GAN] Why Naive Oversampling (SMOTE) Fails on Genomics: Conventional resampling algorithms like SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) generate synthetic samples by taking linear convex combinations between nearest neighbors. In high-dimensional biological data (1,000 gene expression probes), linear interpolation produces biologically impossible vectors that violate non-linear gene co-expression networks and create off-manifold hallucinations. A deep Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) was required to model the underlying manifold geometry of neurotypical control samples.
